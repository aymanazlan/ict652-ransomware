What we know, what they claim, what is unknown

Verified fact

Systems are encrypted

Appointment and patient-record systems are locked. RM30,000 is demanded. An offline backup exists but was not tested for six months. Paper procedures are possible.

Attacker claim

“We copied your records”

The attackers say they copied patient records and that paying will restore access. Neither is proven.

Still unknown

No confirmed evidence of data extraction. We do not know if the backup is clean or how long recovery will take.

04 Solutions · What we agreed

Restore. Keep caring. Don’t pay now.

  1. Test the backup on clean, isolated devices first
  2. Triage and paper procedures keep urgent care going
  3. An honest notice: facts, claims and unknowns
  4. PDPA breach decision within 72 hours

Main limitation

The backup is untested

Test starts within 24 hours. Urgent patients are prioritised or referred. If restoration fails, we review options with specialists, but payment never replaces honest disclosure.

04 Solutions · Q4 record, continued

The hard parts, answered honestly

Payment concern

No ransom without review

No payment until the backup result and specialist advice are in. The owner reviews financial risk within 24 hours.

Protecting patients

Truth plus care

Urgent cases first, a named contact person, regular updates, and the PDP Commissioner and patients told if the breach is notifiable.

Still unresolved

If the backup fails…

The owner may reconsider payment. The IT officer insists on specialist advice first. We record this openly.
