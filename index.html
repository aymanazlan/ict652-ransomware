01 · Main issues · The incident

A Sungai Petani clinic is locked out of its own records

> appointment_system ......... ENCRYPTED
> patient_records ............ ENCRYPTED
> ransom_demand .............. RM30,000
> attacker_claim ............. "records copied" (unverified)
> offline_backup ............. present · restore untested 6 months
> paper_procedures ........... available

Verified

Systems are encrypted, RM30,000 is demanded, an untested offline backup exists and paper procedures can be used.

Claimed by attackers

Patient records have been copied, and paying will restore access.

Still unknown

Whether any data left the clinic, whether the backup is clean, and how long recovery will take.

02 · The ethical dilemma

Pressure

Reopen fast and keep it quiet

Urgent care needs and the clinic’s financial survival push toward paying and calling it “routine maintenance”.

VS

Duty

Recover safely and tell the truth

Truthful communication, patient protection and the uncertain results of paying attackers point the other way.

03 · Main issues · Four roles

Four roles, four competing pressures

Clinic Owner

Arief Najib

Wants to protect income and reputation. Pushes to pay quickly and call it “routine maintenance”.

IT Incident Officer

Muhammad Aiman

Muhammad Syazwan

05 · Analysis · Akhlaq values

Patient records are an amanah, not the clinic’s asset

Amanah

Faithfully fulfil a trust

Leaders are accountable for those under their care. This rules out the “routine maintenance” message and makes the owner bear the cost of safe recovery.

Ihsan

Compassion and excellence in care

Patient safety comes before keeping normal operations, so no consultation goes ahead without the information needed to treat safely.

08 · Effective solutions · Recommendation

Restore from backup, keep care running, don’t pay now

  1. Test the backup on clean, isolated devices before reconnecting anything
  2. Run triage and paper procedures so urgent care continues
  3. Send patients an honest notice: confirmed facts, attacker claims and unknowns
  4. Assess PDPA breach notification within 72 hours

Main limitation

The backup is untested

The backup test starts within 24 hours. Urgent patients are prioritised or referred. If restoration fails, we review options with specialists, but payment never replaces honest disclosure.

10 · Creativity of solutions

Each role owns a five-step protocol

Clinic Owner

Honest Incident Decision Guideline

  • Facts in writing before any decision
  • Specialist and legal review before payment
  • Approve only truthful notices

Check: signed notice versions

IT Incident Officer

Containment and Safe Restoration

  • Isolate and preserve evidence
  • Verify attacker claims through logs
  • Restore only on clean devices

Check: restore report signed before go-live

Clinical Operations

Safe Manual Care Continuity

  • Verify identity, allergies and medicines
  • Triage urgent cases first
  • Second-person check on re-entry

Check: triage log every shift

Patient Rep.

Patient Communication and Support

  • Facts, claims and unknowns in every notice
  • A working contact line
  • A channel to report data misuse

Check: replies within 2 working days

Honesty is part of the recovery.

Restore safely, keep caring for patients, and tell them the truth, even before every answer is known.
